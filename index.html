The question every teacher gets

"We're ready to buy a real piano. Where should we go?"

You get asked it a dozen times a year, and the honest answer has always been awkward. The local dealer who pressures families. The Craigslist upright with a cracked pinblock. The digital that gets outgrown in eighteen months. And if you do send a family somewhere good, the dealer makes the sale and you get a thank-you — at best.

You can't take a commission; it would compromise the recommendation, and you'd never want to. But the recommendation has real value. The Piano America National Scholarship Fund turns that value into something you'd be proud to put on your studio's wall.

Questions teachers ask

Before you say yes.

Do I have to sell anything?
No. You answer the question you're already being asked, and point the family to your page. Piano America handles everything after that — the recording, the inspection report, delivery, tuning, warranty. Nobody from Piano America will ever contact your students or their parents unless they reach out to us.
Who chooses the scholarship recipient?
You do. Merit, need, a student heading to a music program, a student who simply never missed a lesson — your criteria, your call. The only requirement is that the recipient is a current or former student of your studio. We'll help with a simple one-page letter if you want one.
Where does the money actually sit?
Piano America holds every studio's fund in a dedicated scholarship account and reports your balance to you after every sale. The award is paid directly to the student's college or music program, or to the student at the ceremony — you decide which. The money never passes through you.
What if my families only buy one piano a year?
The fund rolls over. One $5,500 piano puts $550 in; the next one tips it past $1,000 and triggers the award. Nothing expires, nothing resets, and there's no minimum to stay in the program.
Does this conflict with the MTNA Code of Ethics?
The program was built so that it wouldn't. The Code's concern is a teacher receiving undisclosed personal gain from a recommendation. Here there is no personal gain — you receive nothing — and nothing is undisclosed, because the fund is printed on your studio page for every family to see. Many teachers still prefer to mention it to families up front, and we encourage that.
Can I recommend other dealers too?
Of course. There's no exclusivity and no contract. If a family finds the right piano somewhere else, that's a good outcome for the student, and that's the point.
What does it cost?
Nothing, ever. Not to join, not to stay, not for the studio page, not for the printed cards. The ten percent comes out of Piano America's side of the sale, not the family's — they pay the same published price as any other buyer.
Who is Piano America?
Piano America LLC is based in Wheeling, West Virginia. It is an authorized Kawai dealer with national distribution of new Kawai digital pianos, and a dealer in restored Yamaha and Kawai acoustic pianos imported from Japan. The founders, Doug Hlad and Stanley Stazenski, have spent eleven years promoting piano sales for Kawai dealers around the country — more than $40 million of pianos — before building a dealership of their own around one idea: that a family should be able to hear and read everything about a piano before they buy it.
